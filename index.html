The Crisis in the Capital Region

The Delhi-NCR Air Pollution Trap

Every winter, Delhi-NCR transforms into a dense particulate basin. Landlocked geography, continuous urban emissions, seasonal agricultural residue burning, and adverse meteorology combine to cause rapid, hazardous air quality spikes.

Vehicular Emissions

Over 12 million registered vehicles in Delhi-NCR, heavy diesel interstate freight corridors, and chronic stop-and-go gridlock discharge massive volumes of fine PM2.5, NO2, and toxic polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons.

Contributes ~30–40% winter PM2.5 →

Industrial Boilers & Plants

Unregulated industrial clusters in Ghaziabad, Faridabad, and Okhla operating coal furnaces, rubber recycling kilns, and diesel generator sets release intense plumes of sulfur dioxide (SO2) and aerosol precursors.

High SO2 & primary black carbon →

Construction & Demolition Dust

Rapid real-estate development across Greater Noida, Gurugram, and Delhi resuspends coarse mineral silica and cement dust (PM10). Unpaved road shoulders magnify mechanical dust generation under vehicle tires.

Dominant source of urban PM10 spikes →

Stubble & Agricultural Burning

Post-harvest paddy straw burning in Punjab and Haryana during October–November produces massive transboundary smoke corridors that travel hundreds of kilometers on north-westerly winds straight into Delhi.

Can surge AQI beyond 450 in 24 hours →

Open Solid Waste Incineration

Spontaneous landfill fires at Ghazipur, Bhalswa, and Okhla dump sites alongside night-time open burning of plastic and leaf litter release toxic dioxins, furans, and carbon monoxide.

Highly toxic volatile organics & CO →

Meteorological Inversion Trap

Even if human emissions stay constant, weather changes everything. Low wind speed (<2 m/s), dropping temperatures, and thermal inversion act as a rigid physical lid, preventing pollutants from escaping into the upper atmosphere.

The fundamental multiplier of pollution →
Atmospheric Physics of Air Pollution

Why Weather Dictates Air Pollution

Air pollution is not a static quantity. The concentration of toxic particles in the air you breathe is governed by the Ventilation Coefficient—the product of wind speed and mixing boundary height.

1. THERMAL INVERSION

A warm air layer covers cold ground air like a ceiling, trapping all smoke at surface level.

2. SURFACE WIND SPEED

Winds < 2 m/s cause catastrophic pollutant stagnation; winds > 5 m/s sweep particles away.

3. RELATIVE HUMIDITY

High humidity (>70%) provides moisture seeds for secondary sulfate and nitrate aerosol formation.

4. MIXING HEIGHT

Shrinks from 1200m in summer to under 300m in winter, cutting atmospheric volume by 75%.

Multi-Modal Data Fusion

Multiple Sources. One Intelligent Platform.

No single sensor can capture the complex air dynamics of a 55,000 km² megalopolis. AirSight integrates five distinct spatial and atmospheric data streams.

1. CAAQMS Ground Stations

Official continuous ambient air quality monitoring stations operated by CPCB, DPCC, and UPPCB providing high-precision reference measurements of PM2.5, PM10, and regulatory gases.

Gold-standard reference ground truth

2. Meteorological Weather Stations

India Meteorological Department (IMD) surface synoptic networks and Doppler weather radars tracking temperature profiles, surface winds, barometric trends, and mixing boundary layers.

Captures atmospheric ventilation volume

3. Earth Observation Satellites

Copernicus Sentinel-5P (TROPOMI) tropospheric column NO2 and INSAT-3D/MODIS Aerosol Optical Depth (AOD) mapping transboundary stubble smoke plumes across the Indo-Gangetic Plain.

Wide-area regional smoke tracking

4. Arterial Traffic Congestion Data

Real-time geospatial traffic speed vectors and bottleneck indicators along Outer Ring Road, DND Flyway, and NH-48 serving as high-resolution vehicular emission proxies.

Dynamic urban emission intensity

5. Multi-Year Historical Meteorological Archives

A decade of coupled historical climate and air quality records for Delhi-NCR, allowing machine learning algorithms to learn recurring diurnal patterns, festive firecracker spikes, and seasonal agricultural burning cycles.

Enables long-horizon supervised feature learning

Atmospheric Chemistry Guide

Understanding Key Atmospheric Pollutants

AirSight models all primary particulate fractions and precursor gases established by the Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) National Ambient Air Quality Standards (NAAQS).

PM2.5 ≤ 2.5 µm

Fine Inhalable Particulate Matter

Combustion particles from diesel engines, stubble burning, and secondary sulfate/nitrate synthesis. Deeply penetrates terminal bronchioles and enters systemic circulation.

CPCB 24h Limit: 60 µg/m³ WHO Limit: 15 µg/m³
PM10 ≤ 10 µm

Coarse Inhalable Particulates

Crushed aggregate, construction demolition dust, unpaved road resuspension, and regional windblown crustal soils. Irritates upper respiratory airways and eyes.

CPCB 24h Limit: 100 µg/m³ WHO Limit: 45 µg/m³
NO2 Oxides of Nitrogen

Nitrogen Dioxide

Formed in high-temperature combustion in diesel commercial transport and gas-fired power plants. Acts as the primary chemical catalyst for ground-level ozone formation.

CPCB 24h Limit: 80 µg/m³ WHO Limit: 25 µg/m³
SO2 Sulfur Compound

Sulfur Dioxide

Emitted by heavy furnace oil, coal-fired brick kilns, and industrial boilers across the NCR perimeter. Readily oxidizes in high humidity to form secondary sulfuric aerosols.

CPCB 24h Limit: 80 µg/m³ WHO Limit: 40 µg/m³
CO Carbon Oxide

Carbon Monoxide

Incomplete combustion from idling vehicular traffic in Delhi traffic bottlenecks and open biomass fires. Reduces oxygen carriage in hemoglobin blood cells.

CPCB 1h Limit: 4 mg/m³ WHO Limit: 4 mg/m³
O3 & VOCs Photochemical Smog

Ground-Level Ozone & VOCs

Secondary photochemical pollutants generated when sunlight reacts with vehicular VOCs and NOx. Peaks during sunny afternoons; highly irritating to pulmonary tissues.

CPCB 8h Limit: 100 µg/m³ WHO Limit: 100 µg/m³
Feature Engineering Pipeline

Robust Real-Time Data Preparation

Raw sensor feeds cannot be fed directly to machine learning models. AirSight enforces an 8-step data preparation pipeline to ensure zero missing gaps and eliminate sensor noise.

STEP 01

Raw Ingestion Stream

Heterogeneous JSON/CSV telemetry from CAAQMS and edge IoT microcontrollers.

STEP 02

Missing Data Imputation

Iterative spatial KNN and temporal spline interpolation to fill communication dropouts.

STEP 03

Outlier Detection

Isolation Forest filtering optical dust spikes and electrical noise artifacts.

STEP 04

Duplicate Handling

Hash-based message deduplication across redundant cellular retry buffers.

STEP 05

Timestamp Alignment

Resampling irregular sensor frequencies onto uniform 15-minute and 1-hour temporal buckets.

STEP 06

Geospatial Mapping

Aligning point sensor coordinates with Sentinel-5P 5.5km satellite grid rasters.

STEP 07

Feature Lag Engineering

Deriving wind direction components (u, v vectors), ventilation coefficients, and diurnal cyclics.

STEP 08

AI/ML Ready Tensor

Normalized, scaled 3D tensors ready for spatial-temporal deep neural batch inference.

Bi-LSTM + Temporal Attention / ST-GNN

Technical Challenges & AirSight Solutions

CHALLENGE 1: SENSOR DRIFT & GAPS

Low-cost sensors suffer from dust occlusion and humidity drift.

Strategy: Dynamic calibration against reference CPCB gold stations + KNN imputation.
CHALLENGE 2: SUDDEN WEATHER SHIFTS

Rapid wind drop or evening cooling causes non-linear smog spikes.

Strategy: Real-time numerical weather integration with physics-guided loss penalization.
CHALLENGE 3: TRANSIENT STUBBLE PULSES

Agricultural burning introduces sudden massive smoke plumes.

Strategy: Ingesting daily satellite fire counts (VIIRS) as dynamic upwind boundary conditions.
